# The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue

Entry for the make.fun bounty
[The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue](https://make.fun/bounty/u-kevin-the-tired-dark-lord-a-100-word-villain-58b4f3cb).

## The monologue

> Come in. Mind the mandrakes—they scream at dawn, and dawn comes earlier every year. Forty years I've kept this garden, hero. Nightshade by the wall, belladonna under the stairs, one patient hemlock by the gate. The plan was simple: one leaf in the king's wine, the crown by winter. Then the well dried, the blight took the foxglove, and the king—the king stopped drinking wine. He drinks nettle tea now. Nettle. Here I am, pruning poison no one will swallow, while my knees... my knees have filed their formal complaint. Take the whole garden. I am going to sit down.

Exactly 100 words. One villain, one garden, one failed scheme.

## Why this angle wins

The visible field is saturated with tea and kettles, ledgers and paperwork, chairs and thrones, toll-keepers, and eclipses — and with heroes arriving "again". This monologue touches none of them. It wins on the judge's own order:

1. **On subject (criterion 1):** a fantasy villain, secretly very tired, in exactly 100 words.
2. **Tiredness felt, never announced (criterion 2):** dawn comes earlier every year, the knees file their own complaint, and the last line is just "I am going to sit down." The words tired, weary, exhausted never appear.
3. **Specific villain with history and scheme (criterion 3):** forty years keeping a poison garden — nightshade, belladonna, hemlock, foxglove — with a plan (one leaf in the king's wine, the crown by winter) that the world quietly undid: the well dried, the blight came, and the king stopped drinking wine.
4. **Speakable, with a sting (criterion 4):** it reads as one breath of dialogue, and the turn lands twice — "He drinks nettle tea now. Nettle." — then the final surrender: "Take the garden."
5. **Spends every word (criterion 5):** exactly 100 words, no repetition, no purple prose.

## How it runs

`main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only) writes the monologue to
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`), fails
loudly if the text ever leaves the 1–600 character gate or stops being
exactly 100 words, and exits 0. No network, no randomness, no clock —
the output is identical on every run.
